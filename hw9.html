Exercise 4 – Put these lines in the correct places (…) in the story

A wise old man called Mula was walking along the road through the woods, when he saw a man walking towards him.

The man was carrying a dirty, old sack. He looked very unhappy, and when Mula asked him why, the man told him his story.

He was all alone, (¹) because his wife had left him, and he was also poor and homeless (²) because he had lost his job, and his house.

'Everything I own is in this old sack', he said. 'My life is miserable.' And he walked off.

(³) After the man had gone a few yards, Mula ran past him, took his sack, and ran into the woods. The man was afraid to follow (⁴) – he had heard it was easy to get lost in these woods.

'Now my life is even more miserable!' he said. And he sat down and cried.

Meanwhile, further down the road, Mula came out of the woods and put the man's sack in the middle of the road. Finally, (⁵) when he had stopped crying, the man set off again…

Correct order: (1) because his wife had left him · (2) because he had lost his job, and his house · (3) After the man had gone a few yards · (4) he had heard it was easy to get lost · (5) when he had stopped crying
Grammar Spot
● Grammar Spot Answers

1. Tenses in bold:

  • "The man was carrying a sack." → Past Continuous
  • "He told Mula his story." → Past Simple
  • "He had lost his job." → Past Perfect

2. "His wife left him. He was all alone." → Expressed as: "He was all alone because his wife had left him."

3. We form the Past Perfect with had + the past participle of the verb.

Discussing Grammar – Differences in meaning
1a When I arrived, she cooked dinner. → I arrived first, then she cooked.
1b When I arrived, she was cooking dinner. → She was in the process of cooking when I arrived.
1c When I arrived, she'd cooked dinner. → When I arrived the cooking had already finished and dinner was ready.
2a He spoke good French because he was living in France. → He lived there for a time and spoke French regularly.
2b He spoke good French because he had lived in France. → He lived there in the past (probably not now) and so his French was good.
3a I listened to music while I did my homework. → Both actions happened at the same time.
3b I listened to music when I'd done my homework. → I did homework first, then listened to music.
4a She gave me a book, and I read it. → She gave me the book first and then I read it.
4b She gave me a book, but I'd read it. → She gave me a book but I had already read it before she gave it to me.
5a When I got home, the children were going to bed. → I arrived home, the children were in their bedrooms, in their pyjamas.
5b When I got home, the children had gone to bed. → I arrived home, the children were in bed and perhaps asleep.

(Ders Kitabı s. 97)
Exercise 1 (9.15) – Which word has most stress?
→ "That was such an amazing film!" — such and so both carry strong stress.
Exercise 3 – When do we use …?
● so / such / such a(n) / so many / so much
  • so + adjective or adverb: I was so surprised! / She sings so beautifully!
  • such a/an + (adjective +) noun (singular): It was such a shock! / such an awful day!
  • such + adjective + plural noun: You have such crazy ideas!
  • such + adjective + uncountable noun: We had such terrible weather!
  • so many + plural noun: There were so many people in town!
  • so much + uncountable noun: I've got so much work!

9.8 – Interview with Liz Davis (extract)
I Now, children's literature is very big business at the moment — is that because of the recent popularity of young adult fiction?
L No, not really. We are in a golden age of children's literature for all ages right now. And it isn't the first golden age — there have been three. The first came in the 19th century, with the earliest fantasy books for children. Alice in Wonderland and Peter Pan were the first novels where ordinary children had adventures in a magical world.
I And until then, children's books had been quite serious and educational, hadn't they?
L Oh yes! People thought children's stories should teach them morals … there's a lovely story called The Storyteller, by a writer called Saki, which makes fun of this idea.
9.11 – The man's story about Bertha (extract)

The young man tells the children the story of Bertha…

"'Once upon a time, a long time ago, there was a little girl called Bertha, who was very, very good.' The children looked disappointed. They had hoped for a better story from him.
"'She was always well-behaved, and worked hard at school because she wanted to please her parents and teachers. She was never late, never dirty or untidy, never rude, and she never told lies.'
"'Was she pretty?' asked the children. 'Not as pretty as you,' the young man said. 'But she was horribly good.'
"The children were suddenly interested. Using the word 'horribly' with good was unusual, and they liked how true it sounded.
"'Bertha was so good that she'd won three medals for it — one said "Never late", one said "Always polite", and the third said "Best-behaved child in town". She wore them every day … so that everyone knew how good she was. "Horribly good," Cyril reminded him.
"'The King had heard how good this girl was, and he invited Bertha to tea at the Royal Palace … But in the woods, there lived a big hungry wolf. He quickly saw Bertha's clean white dress through the trees … Bertha saw the wolf and ran, but it was difficult to run fast because of the medals. She hid in some bushes, but she was shaking with fear, and the wolf could hear the medals clinking together. He caught her, and ate every bit of her — except for the medals, of course.'
